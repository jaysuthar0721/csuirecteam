Our Projects

Every project is a different engineering problem.

Alongside our year-round HPR certification program, Ram Rocketry runs an experimental rocketry track that tackles a new engineering challenge every year.

In Development

Active Fin Stabilization: "Long Shot"

An actively controlled rocket using servo-actuated fins and onboard avionics to demonstrate full 3-axis (roll, pitch, and yaw) control, correcting attitude in flight and optimizing trajectory performance in real time.

This two-phase canard control demonstrator has directly sharpened our team's avionics and control-system capabilities, feeding straight into the design of our IREC 2027 competition vehicle.

Competition Build

Two-Stage Rocket

For the first time in club history, Ram Rocketry is competing in a two-stage rocket challenge: carry the maximum number of USGA-approved golf balls to the maximum altitude possible, while staying under a specific total motor impulse.

Scoring is calculated with a non-linear equation that balances altitude against payload mass, which pushes the team to optimize staging, mass budgeting, and motor selection all at once.

In Development

Glider Rocket

A rocket that transitions mid-flight into a remotely controlled glider. Using fixed wings and onboard avionics, the vehicle achieves controlled, winged recovery instead of a traditional parachute descent.

It's an ambitious departure from standard dual-deploy recovery, and a proving ground for flight-control software and structural design under aerodynamic loads.

Liquid Propulsion Project

Our vision is to establish the first student-led liquid rocket engine program at Colorado State University, focused on rigorous engineering, safe testing practices, and industry-relevant design workflows.

The engine is a bi-propellant, pressure-fed design: 80% ethanol / 20% water fuel with nitrous oxide (N₂O) oxidizer, a pintle injector, ablative thermal protection, and a CNC-machined stainless steel chamber targeting ~1,100 N of thrust.

Liquid Engine Specs

Building CSU's first student-led liquid rocket engine.

Our goal is simple: learn fast, build the engine correctly, and execute safely. Here's the current design baseline, open to iteration as testing progresses.

Program History

Ram Launch Initiative: our NASA USLI program

Before shifting focus to IREC, Ram Rocketry extended into Ram Launch Initiative (RLI), our NASA University Student Launch Initiative senior capstone team. Over two years of competition, RLI students designed and flew a full high-power rocket to NASA standards, finishing 7th out of 67 teams in our debut year. Visit the Ram Launch Initiative site →
