International Rocket Engineering Competition

Our next competition: the Spaceport America Cup.

Ram Rocketry is transitioning its competition program from NASA's University Student Launch Initiative to IREC — the world's largest intercollegiate rocket engineering competition.

Formerly NASA USLI

Why we're making the switch.

For the past several years, Ram Rocketry's competition arm was Ram Launch Initiative (RLI), our senior-capstone team in NASA's University Student Launch Initiative (USLI). RLI was one of the first Colorado university teams to compete in that program, and over two years of competition designed and flew a full high-power rocket to NASA standards — finishing 7th out of 67 teams in our debut year.

That program required an all-senior core team, with underclassmen from Ram Rocketry participating as volunteer interns. It gave the club a strong foundation in full-vehicle design, review milestones, and formal technical reporting.

Ram Rocketry is now transitioning the club's primary competition focus to IREC at the Spaceport America Cup — marking our first entry into IREC in over six years. IREC gives us a fully student-owned vehicle program open to members at every class year, and a bigger, internationally recognized stage to prove what this team can build and fly.

What is IREC?

The International Rocket Engineering Competition is held annually at the Spaceport America Cup, drawing collegiate rocketry teams from across the globe to design, build, and fly high-power and experimental rockets to precise target altitudes, in categories ranging from commercial solid motors to student-built liquid and hybrid propulsion.

It's one of the most competitive proving grounds in collegiate aerospace engineering — and the next chapter for Ram Rocketry.

Our Two-Year Mission Roadmap

Two programs, running in parallel.

Ram Rocketry is executing an ambitious two-year program that builds from a nationally competitive solid-propellant rocket to a fully student-designed liquid propulsion system. The IREC 2027 COTS effort serves as the foundation; the Liquid Propulsion Project is our long-term capability goal.

Phase 1 · June 2027

IREC 2027 — 10,000 ft COTS Category

Ram Rocketry will compete for the first time at the Spaceport America Cup in the 10,000 ft Commercial Off-The-Shelf (COTS) category. Our vehicle is a student-designed high-power rocket targeting a 10,000 ft apogee on a COTS solid motor.

The 2026–2027 build spans the full vehicle: composite airframe, nosecone, fins, dual-deploy parachute recovery, an airbrake system, and onboard avionics/flight computer — backed by detailed flight simulation in OpenRocket, openMotor, and CFD analysis.

Phase 2 · 2027 – 2028

IREC 2028 — Liquid Propulsion Category

In parallel, our Liquid Propulsion team spends 2026–2027 designing, manufacturing, and hot-fire testing a student-built, pressure-fed bi-propellant engine — then integrates it into a flight vehicle for IREC 2028.

Targeting a 10,000 ft apogee on a liquid engine would make Ram Rocketry one of very few collegiate teams to fly a student-designed liquid engine at an international competition.

IREC 2027 Vehicle Scope

What goes into a 10,000 ft competition rocket.

Airframe

Composite airframe, nosecone, and fin design engineered for a 10,000 ft target apogee.

Recovery

Dual-deploy parachute recovery system with fully redundant onboard avionics.

Air Brakes

An active air-braking system for fine altitude control near the target apogee.

Simulation

Flight modeling in OpenRocket and openMotor, backed by CFD analysis on key components.
