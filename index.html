We build and fly high-power rockets.

Ram Rocketry is CSU's collegiate high-power rocketry organization. We're a hands-on team of student engineers who run a High-Power Rocketry certification program, build ambitious engineering projects, and compete nationally and internationally, from NASA's University Student Launch Initiative to the International Rocket Engineering Competition (IREC) and our own student-built Liquid Propulsion Project.

What We Do

Hands-on rocketry and real aerospace engineering experience.

Ram Rocketry is a collegiate rocketry organization built around learning by doing. We welcome everyone, from seasoned high-power enthusiasts to students who are simply curious about space. Members earn High-Power Rocketry certifications and pick up real aerospace engineering experience across a range of ambitious projects.

Certification Program

We run High-Power Rocketry Level 1 & 2 certification flights all year, built around our own in-house, 3D-printable, flight-tested L1 kit.

Real Engineering Skills

MATLAB, Simulink, CAD (SolidWorks & Fusion 360), CFD, FEA, Python, and C++, applied across full rocket design, build, and flight cycles.

Industry Networking

We host site visits and networking events with companies including Lockheed Martin, United Launch Alliance, Sierra Space, and Ursa Major.

Two-Year Mission Roadmap

What's ahead over the next two years.

Phase 1 · 2026–2027

IREC 2027: 10,000 ft COTS Solid Rocket

A full, student-designed high-power rocket, complete with airframe, nosecone, fins, dual-deploy recovery, and an airbrake system, flying on a commercial off-the-shelf solid motor toward a 10,000 ft target apogee at IREC in Midland, Texas.

Phase 2 · 2026–2028

Liquid Propulsion Engine Development & Static Fire

In parallel, our Liquid Propulsion team is designing, manufacturing, and hot-fire testing a student-built, pressure-fed bi-propellant engine (ethanol/N₂O) with a pintle injector and ablative cooling.

Phase 2 · 2028

IREC 2028: Liquid-Propelled Rocket Flight

Integrating our validated engine into a flight vehicle and competing at IREC, targeting a 10,000 ft apogee. That would make Ram Rocketry one of very few collegiate teams flying a student-designed liquid engine internationally.
